## Company Structure
JobCloud is a two-sided marketplace. 
- B2B side - also referred to as "Core" (product, engineering, UX) (owns activation, workflows, integrations, notifications). B2B focuses on the products for the recruiters: how to publish a job ad? where? when? how? Pricing? How to manage the applications recevied? 
- B2C (product, engineering, UX) focus on the products for the job seekers: search bar, seeker profile, apply form. 
- Data and analytics team ensure the correct tracking of online behavior (Adobe Analytics) and reporting (Tableau). 
- Got-to-market teams (sales, marketing, customer success, operations and support) are organized per sompany segments (SE = 0-100 employees and LME 100+ employees). 

## B2B strucutre 
This team is divided into 
- Domain Team: in charge of the JobCloud ONE platform, except the account creation, online shop and the job ad creation process (User Management, Account Management, applications analysis and management). 
- Integrations Team: in charge of the ingestion of job ads (crawlers and stuff) and the correct integration of external ATSes (sending the applications to the right place). 
- PRO teams: in charge of the Pay per Performance pricing model of the job ad placement. 
Your main focus is the Domain Team but you can keep a broader vision in the background.

## Competitive Landscape
### Direct Competitors
- Indeed (CH): strong traffic and global brand; pricing pressure; [DIFFERENTIATION in favor of Jobcloud: local depth, higher-quality Swiss applicants, language/canton targeting]
- LinkedIn Jobs: strong professional network and sourcing; [DIFFERENTIATION in favor of Jobcloud:: local focus, better ad ROI for roles requiring Swiss market reach]

## Product Philosophy (not the right information)
### Core Beliefs
1. Context over status updates
- Tasks should have enough context that anyone can pick them up
- Status meetings are waste - status should be visible in the tool
2. Async by default
- Don't require synchronous coordination
- Build for teams across timezones
3. Opinionated but flexible
- Strong defaults (workflows that work)
- Customizable when needed (not overwhelming)
4. Speed matters
- Tools should be fast to learn, fast to use, fast to load
- No loading spinners, no lag
5. Beautiful and functional
- Good design isn't just aesthetics
- UI should guide users to success
